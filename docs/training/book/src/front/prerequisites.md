# Learner Profile & Prerequisites

## Who this course is for

A student who has just finished **Class 12** (any stream — Science, Commerce, or
Arts) and wants a structured, exam-credentialed entry into Indian capital
markets without waiting for a three-year degree. No prior finance, accounting,
or statistics coursework is required.

## Prerequisite matrix (Week-0 entry gate)

| Domain | Required minimum | Gap-closing action |
|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic | Fractions, percentages, ratios, exponents, negative numbers | S1-M1 math bootcamp + nightly drills |
| Algebra | Solving a linear equation for one unknown | S1-M1 remediation labs |
| Statistics | **None required** — taught from zero | S1-M1 (descriptive stats), S4-M1 (inferential) |
| English | Basic reading/writing | Weekly communication lab (S1-M9) |
| Computing | None; mouse/keyboard comfort is enough | S1-M2 doubles as computing bootcamp |
| Finance | **None required** | Whole of Semester 1 is the bridge |
| Trading | **None required** | S2-M8 trading terminal & paper lab |

## What a high-school passout needs that a graduate has

Graduate programmes (e.g., the NISM PGP source syllabus) assume you already know:
basic statistics, spreadsheet literacy, how a company's accounts work, and the
language of markets. This course rebuilds all four in **Semester 1** before
layering the PGP-trimester content in Semesters 2–4. That "bridge + ladder"
design is what makes the course robust for a non-graduate cohort.

## Week-0 skills audit

On day one you will sit a 60-minute diagnostic covering:

1. Arithmetic & percentages (15 Q)
2. Basic algebra (10 Q)
3. Reading comprehension in English (10 Q)
4. Simple data-reading (tables and charts) (10 Q)
5. A one-page written self-introduction (essay)

The diagnostic is **not a filter** — it calibrates your remediation plan.
Scores below the line in any domain add extra lab hours in S1-M1/M2.

How to read your scores:

- **1–2 in any row** — plan the S1-M1/M2 remediation for that domain and budget
  3–4 extra lab hours a week there for the first month.
- **3** — fine; keep the nightly/weekly drills the module assigns.
- **4–5** — skip the remediation; consider pairing with a lab-buddy who rated
  that row low.

Keep this worksheet; you will re-score it at the end of Semester 1 and the
improvement is part of your journal.

## What to expect in the first two weeks

Week 1 is the steepest — and often the most rewarding — week of the course.

**Week 1.** The 60-minute diagnostic on day one, then the S1-M1 math bootcamp
begins (percentages and ratios, not abstract theory). On day two or three comes
your **install day**: Python 3.12, the `tantra` wheel, and running
`tantra-examples/examples/01_loading_data.py` so that *your* screen prints 250
synthetic bars. You also open your journal and write your first entry — even if
it is "I do not yet know what a candlestick is."

**Week 2.** Linear equations, then the S1-M2 computing module doubles as a
spreadsheet bootcamp: you build your first SUM/AVERAGE table and your first
indicator plot from synthetic data. Your first graded lab and the S1-M1 reading
assignment are due at the end of the week.

Two honest notes. First, it is *normal* to feel lost on day three — every new
term is supposed to be new, and Semester 1 assumes exactly that. Second, do not
skip the journal in week 1. The journal habit is the skill the whole programme
is built on; start it on day one, not day thirty.

## Tools you need before day one

- **A laptop or desktop** (any OS) with 4+ GB RAM and internet for installs.
- **Python 3.12** — check with `python --version` before the install day.
- **The `tantra` wheel and `tantra-examples`** — follow [Tantra Platform & Lab
  Environment](tantra-environment.md) for the exact install commands and the
  smoke test. If `maturin` gives you trouble, ask a mentor for a prebuilt wheel
  — do *not* skip the smoke test.
- **A spreadsheet app** — LibreOffice Calc (free) or Microsoft Excel. Needed
  from S1-M2 and heavily in S3 for the modeled company.
- **A text editor** — VS Code (free) is enough; anything with line numbers works.
- **A journal** — a paper notebook or a notes app; one page a day from Week 1.
- **By end of S2 only:** accounts at nism.ac.in and at IFTA/Pearson VUE for
  booking exams (see [Certification Ladder](certifications.md)). Nothing to do
  on day one.

## The learning contract

Every learner signs a learning contract committing to:

- 30+ hrs/week of study, of which ≥ 10 are supervised labs
- Maintaining a **paper-trading journal** from Semester 2 onward
- Attempting **every certification mock** on schedule (see
  [Certification Ladder](certifications.md))
- Sitting both **mid-term and end-term examinations every semester**
  (per the IFMC syllabus discipline, which mandates mid/end-term exams)

## Realistic commitment

The programme is full-time in its default variant. For part-time, evening, or
online variants, see the adaptions table in the curriculum design document
(`docs/training/curriculum-high-school-passout.md`, §11).

## Frequently asked questions about prerequisites

1. **I struggled with Class 12 maths. Can I manage?** Yes. The bar is the
   arithmetic in the matrix above, not your board marks. S1-M1 re-teaches the
   handful of operations markets actually use, and the Week-0 audit tells you
   exactly which rows to remediate first.
2. **I studied Arts/Humanities. Is finance "not for me"?** The prerequisite
   matrix has no stream column because there is no stream requirement. Finance
   is a vocabulary and a set of arithmetic habits — both are taught from zero in
   Semester 1.
3. **I can only study part-time. Can I still join?** The default variant is
   full-time, but the adaptations table in the curriculum design document
   (`docs/training/curriculum-high-school-passout.md`, §11) covers part-time,
   evening, and online variants. The gates and grading stay the same; the
   calendar stretches.
4. **I do not own a laptop.** The programme assumes one from Week 1 — labs,
   mocks, and the journal all run on the Tantra platform. If you need a machine,
   raise it at admission; it is a working tool, not a luxury, and the earlier a
   mentor knows, the more options there are.
