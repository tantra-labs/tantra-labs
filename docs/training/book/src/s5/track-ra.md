## Track modules

### Module 1 — Advanced valuation & modeling (weeks 67–70)

The capstone's spine is a **three-statement model** (income statement, balance
sheet, cash flow) built from actual company history, projected forward, and
valued. This module takes the model you built in S3 and makes it defensible:
your assumptions must be **scenario-tested** (base / bull / bear), your
discount rate must be justified from the CAPM work, and every cross-company
comparable must be chosen with a reason, not convenience. You will also learn
**SOTP (sum-of-the-parts)** discipline — valuing a conglomerate by valuing each
segment separately and adding them, then reconciling to the single-market-price
thesis.

**Builds on:** [S3-M4 ratios](../s3/m4-ratios-dupont.md), [S3-M5 TVM & capital budgeting](../s3/m5-tvm-capital-budgeting.md), [S3-M6 cost of capital](../s3/m6-cost-of-capital-capm.md), [S3-M7 valuation methods](../s3/m7-valuation-methods.md), [S3-M8 financial modeling](../s3/m8-financial-modeling.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) a rebuilt three-statement model with a working scenario
tab (Base/Bull/Bear), (2) a DCF with explicit WACC and terminal-value
assumptions, (3) a comps table of 5–8 peers with justifications, (4) an SOTP
bridge if your company has more than one business segment.

### Module 2 — Technical overlay in reports (weeks 69–72)

A research report is a **fundamental document**, but it is read by traders who
care about entry timing. This module teaches the *overlay*, not the takeover:
chart context (trend, key support/resistance, volume) that tells the reader
when the fundamental thesis is likely to be recognized by the market. The rule
you must internalize: **the technical section can refine the view, never
reverse it.** If your DCF target is ₹1,200 but the chart shows a broken
support, you lower the conviction and widen the target range — you do not delete
the fundamental thesis.

**Builds on:** [S2-M1 Dow theory](../s2/m1-dow-theory.md), [S2-M3 chart patterns](../s2/m3-chart-patterns.md), [S2-M4 indicator math](../s2/m4-indicators-math.md), [S2-M6 advanced indicators](../s2/m6-advanced-indicators.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) a one-page chart note on your capstone company (trend,
support/resistance, key levels, volume), (2) a written rule for how chart
evidence does or does not alter the fundamental rating, (3) a recorded
self-review explaining two levels where you would *not* act on the chart.

### Module 3 — Report writing & compliance deep-dive (weeks 71–74)

SEBI holds research analysts to a specific disclosure regime, and a publish-grade
report looks professional because of its **structure and disclosures**, not
just its math. This module covers the anatomy of a real sell-side report (the
teaser, the summary, the thesis, the model, the risks, the annexes), conflict
declarations (does the analyst, or the desk, own the stock?), rating language
(Buy/Accumulate/Hold/Reduce/Sell and what each means), and peer review — you
will read, critique, and be critiqued on real report drafts.

**Builds on:** [S4-M9 research report writing](../s4/m9-research-report-writing.md), [S4-M10 regulatory & ethics](../s4/m10-regulatory-ethics.md), [S4-M11 NISM XV sprint](../s4/m11-nism-sprint.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) a compliance & disclosure annex for your capstone company
(holdings, conflicts, rating definitions, methodology), (2) two peer reviews of
classmates' drafts using the same rubric as the capstone, (3) a one-page
personal writing-style guide (your house rules for numbers, hedging language,
and citations).

## Capstone tasks

Complete these in order; each task has a deliverable that is graded.

1. **Choose and fix the company** — one NSE/BSE-listed company with ≥ 3 years of
   published financials and at least two reportable business segments.
   *Deliverable:* one-paragraph thesis statement plus a reason the company is
   research-worthy.
2. **Build the history tab** — 5 years of income statement, balance sheet, and
   cash flow, entered in `Decimal` with every number traceable to a filed
   source. *Deliverable:* history tab + source log (statement, page, line).
3. **Forecast three scenarios** — Base/Bull/Bear with 3-year projections and
   explicit drivers (revenue growth, margin, capex, working capital).
   *Deliverable:* scenario tab with the driver table.
4. **Value the company three ways** — DCF with WACC and terminal-value
   assumptions; multiples against the comps set; SOTP if multi-segment.
   *Deliverable:* valuation tab with a target range (low–high), not a single
   false-precision number.
5. **Write the report** — the six-section publish-grade structure with the
   technical overlay note placed in its own short section.
   *Deliverable:* full report draft.
6. **Add the risk plan and disclosures** — every material risk from S4-M7 plus
   the compliance annex from Module 3. *Deliverable:* risk & disclosure annex.
7. **Peer-review two classmates' drafts**, and revise your own in response to
   their reviews. *Deliverable:* revision memo (what you changed and why).
8. **Rehearse and deliver the 15-minute presentation**, then take the panel's
   Q&A. *Deliverable:* defense recording + the final submitted PDF and workbook.

## Grading rubric

| Criterion | Exceeds (A) | Meets (B/C) | Below (D/F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Follows the publish-grade template exactly; every section advances the thesis; the reader never has to hunt for a number | Template followed with minor deviations; sections present but some transitions are weak | Missing sections or ordering that buries the thesis; annexes absent |
| **Evidence / math correctness** | Every claim traced to a filed source or a live computation; comps, DCF, and SOTP reconcile to the same target range | Most claims sourced; one or two untraced figures; model arithmetic consistent | Assertions without sources; valuation methods disagree with no reconciliation |
| **Decimal / exactness** | All money, prices, returns, and ratios are `Decimal`; quantities round consistently to ₹/share and paise | Decimal used throughout with a couple of cosmetic float-format slips | Float-typed money anywhere; rounding that hides a material difference |
| **Risk & disclosure** | Material risks ranked with likelihood/impact; conflicts declared; rating language matches definitions | Risk section present, conflicts named; some risks generic or unranked | Risks boilerplate; conflicts undeclared; rating ungrounded |
| **Presentation** | 15-minute defense is rehearsed, within time, charts legible; answers Q&A with the model at hand | Clear delivery with minor time overrun; most questions answered competently | Reading from slides; unable to defend a key assumption under Q&A |

## Mock sprint

## Tantra Lab

### Lab goal

Prove that every valuation number in your capstone is **reproducible from a
verified screen and a Decimal DCF re-run** — screen the universe, pick the
candidate, and recompute the enterprise value from scratch.

### Code

First run the verified screener to place your company in context:

```bash
python tantra-examples/examples/screening/04_intelligent_investor_screener.py
python tantra-examples/examples/screening/01_multibagger_screener.py
```

Then re-run the DCF in Decimal — this mirrors the S3-M7 method with fresh,
sourced assumptions:

```python
from decimal import Decimal

# Assumptions: sourced, in the journal, all Decimal
fcf = Decimal("100.00")          # Year-1 FCF (₹ crore)
growth = Decimal("0.12")         # 5-year growth
wacc = Decimal("0.11")           # discount rate
years = 5
tv_ebitda = Decimal("12")        # exit multiple on terminal FCF

pv_fcfs = Decimal("0")
for t in range(1, years + 1):
    fcf_t = fcf * (Decimal(1) + growth) ** t
    pv_fcfs += fcf_t / (Decimal(1) + wacc) ** t

tv = fcf * (Decimal(1) + growth) ** years * tv_ebitda
pv_tv = tv / (Decimal(1) + wacc) ** years
ev = pv_fcfs + pv_tv

print("PV of 5-year FCFs   ≈", pv_fcfs)
print("PV of terminal value≈", pv_tv)
print("Enterprise value    ≈", ev)   # add net cash, subtract net debt, divide by shares
```

### Expected output

A print-out on the order of: PV of FCFs ≈ ₹513.68 cr, PV of terminal value ≈
₹1,255.04 cr, enterprise value ≈ ₹1,768.72 cr (before net cash/net debt and
per-share division). The point is not the exact numbers — it is that changing
any one Decimal assumption and re-running reproduces the model, which is the
discipline the panel will probe.

### Hand-in checklist

- [ ] Screener run logged with the candidate's rank in the output.
- [ ] DCF re-run matches the workbook's valuation tab to the last paisa.
- [ ] WACC, growth, and exit-multiple assumptions cited to a source.
- [ ] Journal entry: one paragraph on what the screen added to the thesis.

### Common errors

- **Mixing float into the screen export** — always convert screen output to
  `Decimal` before it enters the model.
- **Terminal value in the wrong year** — the exit multiple applies to the
  *terminal* year cash flow, discounted for `years` periods, not `years − 1`.
- **Forgetting net cash / net debt** — enterprise value is not equity value;
  the bridge must be explicit.
