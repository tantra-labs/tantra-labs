## Track modules

### Module 1 — Overview of financial planning & wealth management (weeks 67–70)

Financial planning starts with the **person**, not the product: what does the
client want, over what horizon, with what tolerance for loss? This module
teaches the planning process end to end — gather facts (income, expenses,
assets, liabilities, dependants, goals), **risk-profile** the client (a
questionnaire that maps attitude to a conservative/moderate/aggressive
bucket), define goals with money values and dates, and write the **plan
document**: the written output the client signs off. The professional rule you
will live by: *a recommendation with no stated client goal is not a plan, it is
a sale.*

**Builds on:** [S3-M1 economics](../s3/m1-economics.md), [S3-M3 financial statement analysis](../s3/m3-financial-statement-analysis.md), [S3-M5 TVM & capital budgeting](../s3/m5-tvm-capital-budgeting.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) a blank client fact-finder and risk-profile questionnaire,
(2) a plan-document template with the seven standard sections, (3) a completed
sample fact-finder for the case-study family (provided in Week 66).

### Module 2 — Building blocks of FP/WM (weeks 69–72)

The toolkit, in the order a plan uses it: **emergency fund** (6 months of
expenses, liquid and safe), **insurance** (term life for income protection,
health cover — risk transfer before risk-taking), **debt** (which liabilities
to retire first), **investing** (equity, debt, mutual funds, and the 
**product-suitability** question: does this product fit *this* client's risk
profile, horizon, and goals), **retirement** (the corpus required at a target
age), and **estate** (nomination, will, and beneficiary planning). Each block
has a standard calculation — emergency-fund sizing, human-life-value for term
cover, SIP accumulation for goals — and each calculation is Decimal math you
must be able to reproduce from memory.

**Builds on:** [S4-M3 portfolio theory](../s4/m3-portfolio-theory.md), [S4-M8 currency & fixed income](../s4/m8-currency-fixed-income.md), [S3-M7 valuation methods](../s3/m7-valuation-methods.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) the six building-block worksheets with worked Decimal
examples for the case-study family, (2) a product-suitability matrix
(see Tantra Lab), (3) a one-page "product map" — which NSE/MF categories serve
which goals.

### Module 3 — Risk management, practice & regulation (weeks 71–74)

Advising is regulated precisely because clients trust you with their money.
This module covers the **SEBI Investment Adviser regulations**: who may call
themselves an investment adviser, the **suitability** obligation (recommend
only what fits the client, and document why), **fee disclosure** (fee
structures and the conflicts that come with them), **KYC/AML** obligations
(know your client, verify identity, report suspicious activity), and the
**record-keeping** duty (every recommendation, its basis, and the client's
suitability profile on file). The compliance reflex is simple and unforgiving:
if you cannot show why a product suited the client at the time, you have no
case.

**Builds on:** [S4-M10 regulatory & ethics](../s4/m10-regulatory-ethics.md), [S4-M11 NISM sprint](../s4/m11-nism-sprint.md).

**Deliverables:** (1) a suitability-and-compliance annex template, (2) a KYC/AML
checklist for onboarding a new client, (3) a fee-disclosure statement with two
fee structures compared in Decimal.

## Capstone

Write a **client financial plan** for a case-study family:

- Client risk profile → asset allocation recommendation.
- Product mapping (equity/debt/MF/insurance) with rationale.
- Suitability and compliance annex (why each product fits the client).
- A 10-year projection using SIP/return math.

## Capstone tasks

Complete these in order; each task has a deliverable that is graded.

1. **Read the case study** — a family fact-finder is provided: ages, income,
   expenses, savings, debts, dependants, and two or three stated goals.
   *Deliverable:* a goals table — each goal with a money value, a date, and a
   priority.
2. **Risk-profile the client** — run the questionnaire and justify the bucket.
   *Deliverable:* the risk profile with the questionnaire answers.
3. **Sweep the household balance sheet** — assets, liabilities, income, and
   expense surplus in Decimal. *Deliverable:* net-worth statement + monthly
   surplus line.
4. **Fix the safety blocks first** — emergency fund and insurance cover sized
   and costed. *Deliverable:* emergency-fund and term-cover worksheets.
5. **Set the asset allocation** — the strategic equity/debt split from the
   risk profile, with the [S4-M3](../s4/m3-portfolio-theory.md) logic cited.
   *Deliverable:* allocation recommendation with the reasoning.
6. **Map products to goals** — equity/debt/MF/insurance products, each with a
   suitability reason. *Deliverable:* the product-mapping section.
7. **Project 10 years** — SIP/return math for each goal, in Decimal (see
   Tantra Lab). *Deliverable:* the projection workbook and projection table.
8. **Write the suitability & compliance annex** — why each product fits, fee
   disclosure, KYC/AML check, records plan. *Deliverable:* the annex.
9. **Record the 15-minute case defense** — present the plan as to the family.
   *Deliverable:* recording + final plan document.

## Grading rubric

| Criterion | Exceeds (A) | Meets (B/C) | Below (D/F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Goals → risk profile → safety → allocation → products → projection → compliance flows in professional plan order | Correct order with minor gaps; some sections thin | Plan missing goals or the suitability annex; order obscures the client's situation |
| **Evidence / math correctness** | Every goal sized to a shown calculation; projections reconcile with the stated return assumptions; insurance/emergency sizing sourced | Most calculations shown; one or two asserted | Projections asserted with no arithmetic; goals with no money value or date |
| **Decimal / exactness** | All money, returns, and projections are `Decimal`; annual numbers tie to monthly worksheets exactly | Decimal throughout with minor formatting slips | Float-typed money or returns; projection that doesn't tie to its inputs |
| **Risk & disclosure** | Suitability reason for every product; fee structure disclosed; risks of each product stated against the client's profile | Suitability and fees present; some products only generically justified | No suitability logic; fees undisclosed; products recommended without a client fit |
| **Presentation** | 15-min defense within time, plan read as a narrative, compliance questions answered from the annex | Clear delivery, minor time overrun, most questions answered | Reading from the plan; unable to defend a product recommendation under Q&A |

## Tantra Lab

### Lab goal

Prove the 10-year projection and the suitability matrix for the case-study
family — the two numbers-heavy heart of the plan — are reproducible, in Decimal,
from the verified SIP tooling.

### Code

First run the verified SIP backtest for the plan's savings projections:

```bash
python tantra-examples/examples/mutual_funds/02_sip_backtest.py
python tantra-examples/examples/mutual_funds/03_category_analysis.py
```

Then reproduce the annuity-due SIP math in Decimal, so you can show the family
exactly how the corpus was built:

```python
from decimal import Decimal

monthly = Decimal("10000")        # SIP instalment (₹)
months = Decimal(120)             # 10-year horizon
annual_rate = Decimal("0.12")     # assumed annual return
r = annual_rate / Decimal(12)     # monthly rate

fv = Decimal("0")
for m in range(1, int(months) + 1):
    fv += monthly * (Decimal(1) + r) ** m   # annuity due: invested at period start

print("Total invested          ≈", monthly * months)
print("Corpus after 10 years   ≈", fv)
print("Wealth gained           ≈", fv - monthly * months)
```

### Expected output

For a ₹10,000/month SIP at 12% p.a. over 10 years: about ₹12,00,000 invested,
a corpus of approximately ₹23,23,000, and wealth gained of roughly ₹11,23,000.
The exact figures depend on the return assumption you state in the plan — the
point is that the worksheet and the projection workbook must agree to the last
paisa.

### The suitability matrix

Build this table in the plan document — it is the backbone of the suitability
annex:

| Client risk profile | Emergency fund | Insurance | Equity allocation | Debt allocation | Example products |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | 6 months' expenses, liquid | Term + health, before investing | 20–30% (index funds) | 70–80% (debt funds, FD ladder) | Liquid/ultra-short fund, short-duration fund |
| **Moderate** | 6 months' expenses, liquid | Term + health, before investing | 40–50% (diversified equity) | 50–60% (corporate-bond/balanced) | Diversified equity MF, corporate-bond fund |
| **Aggressive** | 6 months' expenses, liquid | Term + health, before investing | 60–80% (multi-cap/small-cap sleeve) | 20–40% (gilt/credit-quality blend) | Multi-cap fund, small-cap fund (sleeve only) |

### Hand-in checklist

- [ ] SIP backtest run and logged with the assumed return rate.
- [ ] Decimal projection worksheet matches the workbook to the paisa.
- [ ] Suitability matrix filled with the family's profile and product mapping.
- [ ] Fee-disclosure statement and KYC/AML checklist filed in the annex.
- [ ] Journal entry: which product was hardest to justify, and how you handled it.

### Common errors

- **Annuity ordinary vs due** — SIPs invest at the *start* of the period; use
  the annuity-due form as above, not the ordinary-annuity form.
- **Annual rate used as monthly** — convert `annual_rate / 12` first; a 12% p.a.
  rate is a 1% monthly rate, and mixing them inflates the corpus.
- **Float anywhere in the projection** — the whole workbook must be `Decimal`;
  a `float` return assumption invalidates the plan's numbers.
